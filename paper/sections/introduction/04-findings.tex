I find that the pre-registered H2 ordering holds, while the tested persona
mechanism does not explain it (\reffig{headline}). The
pre-registered H2 prediction was nine cross-class relations over five
behaviors, with \behavior{both\_sides} and \behavior{ethical\_frameworks}
as an unordered final equivalence class. All nine relations held at 25\%
removal. Under the declared all-five rank-permutation frame, $2/120 = 0.0167$
is the nominal rank-tail. Two effects were already known when the final set
was fixed, so this is not a fully fresh five-effect $p$-value; no calibrated
conditional $p$-value or selection model is recorded, and the target retrains
are single-seed. The measured separability index does not itself rank the
effects:
\behavior{validate\_feelings} is fourth by separability but second by
filtering effect.

The negative result is narrower and more informative than a universal
persona claim. H1 and H3 test the pre-registered $k=4$, layer-22,
frozen/post-training linear persona-bundle instantiation: the document
rankings do not collapse along its persona lines, and removing its persona
component does not rescue filtering. P3a did not meet its registered
improvement criterion for \behavior{validate\_feelings}, while P3b's
pre-registered equivalence bound holds in one seed for
\behavior{both\_sides}. Separately, H4 scores a 5,666,170-document subset of a
5,678,621-row manifest across 577 bins; it is a localization result and
search prior, not a causal explanation of H2.
