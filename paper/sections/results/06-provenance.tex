H4 asks where in real pretraining data the persona direction gets its
support. The scoring path and concentration statistic were logged before
the corresponding numbers were seen: score the 5{,}666{,}170-document subset
of the 5{,}678{,}621-row manifest with the frozen persona axis, plus 20
matched-norm random directions as a finite null sample, and measure how
concentrated each direction's top-scoring decile is across the 577
WebOrganizer format/topic bins (Gini coefficient; higher = more
concentrated). The pre-registration anticipated 576 bins; materializing the
frozen manifest yielded 577 distinct bins, all of which were retained.
Observed: $G(\text{axis}) = 0.652$ versus a null 95th
percentile of $0.557$ and a null maximum of $0.581$; the axis exceeds all 20
null draws (\reffig{provenance}). The finite draw count does not support a
population-tail claim. The top decile shows the predicted
interpersonal/instructional pattern: \texttt{q\_a\_forum} carries 18.7\%
of the axis's top-decile mass (4.4x its corpus rate),
\texttt{comment\_section} 16.5\% (3.9x), then \texttt{faq} and
\texttt{customer\_support} (about 2.2--2.3x); the top individual bins are
Q\&A and comment bins across otherwise unrelated topics (enrichment
6.3--8.2x). The null Ginis are themselves 0.38--0.58, since any direction
concentrates somewhat as bins differ in size and content, which is why the
verdict is against the random-direction null rather than against
uniformity. H4 is a generic persona-axis localization map and search
prior; it does not test whether these bins carry a target behavior, cause
its filtering effect, or explain the H2 ordering.
