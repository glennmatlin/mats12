The comment that proposed the persona-bundle model asked how one might
falsify it; I tested that falsification route and report the descriptive
alignment and mechanism results separately.

The frozen persona basis aligns descriptively with the tested behaviors. In
this checkpoint and basis, every tested behavior direction has roughly
5--10x the matched random-direction load, but that shared alignment does
not distinguish the elicited and taught groups. The generic persona axis
has concentrated attribution support in Q\&A and comment formats, with 4.4x
and 3.9x enrichment relative to their corpus shares, respectively, and is
above the random-direction null (H4). This is a search map, not evidence
that those bins induce a target behavior or explain the H2 ordering.

The pre-registered $k=4$, layer-22, frozen/post-training linear
persona-bundle instantiation does not explain the ordering. Unfilterable
behaviors are not deeper
in that subspace (H1, contrast reversed), and subtracting the subspace from
the ranking barely changes it (H3). A recent causal training-time
intervention in a different emergent-misalignment setup shows why this does
not rule out persona-mediated learning in other models, bases, or timings
\citep{nadaf2026persona}.

The low overlap between the two \behavior{both\_sides} scorer pipelines'
top-quarter sets motivates checking what their rankings capture. I leave
support coherence untested until the sampled documents receive personal
review and a prospective test. The flat control's highest persona load
alongside no detectable fine-tuning effect in this sample is compatible
with saturation, but this study does not measure saturation directly.
