The anchor post reports that deleting the quarter of training documents most
implicated in a behavior can erase refusal while leaving both-sides framing
intact \citep{lee2026filtering}. This reconstruction tests one proposed
persona explanation for that split on the
\texttt{allenai/Olmo-3-1025-7B} checkpoint with 14 unique filter-retrain cells,
including the matched-method \behavior{both\_sides} probe baseline. The
pre-registered H2 test was fixed before its separability, overlap, and three
added target-cell outcomes were viewed: five behaviors, nine cross-class
relations, and a final equivalence class for \behavior{both\_sides} and
\behavior{ethical\_frameworks}. All nine relations held at 25\% removal;
under the declared all-five rank-permutation frame, $2/120 = 0.0167$ is the
nominal rank-tail. Two effects were already known when the final set was
fixed, so this is not a fully fresh five-effect $p$-value; no calibrated
conditional $p$-value or selection model is recorded, and the target
retrains are single-seed. The
tested object was a frozen, post-training $k=4$, layer-22 linear
persona-bundle instantiation. Its H1 collapse test was not supported and H3
P3a did not meet its registered improvement criterion for
\behavior{validate\_feelings}; H3 P3b stayed within its pre-registered
equivalence band in one seed for \behavior{both\_sides}. Separately, H4 scores a
5,666,170-document subset of a 5,678,621-row manifest across 577 bins.
That is a localization map and search prior, not evidence that those bins
cause a target behavior or explain the H2 ordering.
